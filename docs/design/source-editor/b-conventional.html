The Six Minds of Experience

A common definition of wayfinding is that it is about getting from point A to point B. For the products you build it is richer than that: it is the sum of every cue that tells someone where they are, what is around them, and how to get back.

MindThe question it answers
Vision / AttentionWhat should I look at first?
WayfindingWhere am I, and how do I get where I am going?
MemoryHave I seen this before?

Vision, Attention, and Automaticity

Attention is a spotlight with a narrow beam and a very short memory. Anything the interface expects a person to find by searching has already cost them something they cannot get back.

The most expensive words in an interface are the ones a person has to read twice.

Applying it to a reading surface

  • One column, one measure, one place the eye can rest.
  • Landmarks at every scale: a title, a section, a paragraph, a line.

Buxton B — contextual, conventional. Nothing here has to be learned: a title, tabs, an outline, an inspector — the schema of every documentation tool. Everything about the source is visible at once, which is its real virtue for a first-time reader. Its costs are the ones this redesign exists to remove: the text is back inside a card, the inspector takes 268px of the centre by default, the outline rail takes 212px more, and “reading the content” becomes a tab — one of four equal choices, two of which are inert while you read.
